# Round 2: implications for information-budget prediction

All 55 fineQComp cells completed: strength/duration 8, support frontiers 20, cue bridge 27. The audit reads predictions and every rescored codec, not just selected summaries. See [the numerical audit](round2_audit_2026_09_10.json).

**The rate target remains the first constraint.** At 97% teacher decision agreement on the 426 test examples whose labels occurred in training, directly trained rank-1 students need about 0.745 MB for Mistral; recompressing rank-16 students needs about 5.149 MB, a 6.9× gap. Qwen gives 0.705 MB versus 1.297–2.507 MB. The same grid generally cannot reach that fidelity on the 74 examples with unseen labels. These are descriptive test frontiers, not prospective predictions, and label agreement does not establish full-distribution equivalence. They show why both training route and target support must remain visible when interpreting a compressed adapter's size as information.

**The late-switch improvement mostly vanishes after separating completed prefixes.** For permuted-rationale adapters, quarter strength improves test accuracy from 5.4% to 37.2% on Mistral and 11.8% to 79.1% on Qwen. Switching back to full strength at token 128 seems to retain 19.6%/41.6% accuracy. But 109/296 Mistral prefixes and 118/296 Qwen prefixes already terminated before that switch. On continuations that actually switch, accuracy is only 4.3%/13.5%. This does not support a strong “correct early course, then safe full-strength continuation” claim. The live-prefix subsets depend on the prefix policy, so they are descriptive decompositions, not a new randomized comparison.

**The cue bridge separates available rule information from learned access.** At 8 programs, hidden-only, cue-then-hidden and mixed curricula score 28.4%, 29.2% and 28.9% on uncued test inputs. At 16 programs they score 7.0%, 9.1% and 10.7%. Mixed training retains near-perfect correct-cue performance while following a wrong cue on almost every discordant case. More training schedules are a lower priority than measuring what a particular learner can acquire.

The next array therefore focuses on receiver-relative codes and calibrated byte prediction. It compares frozen-context coding, early online excess coding, cross-half transfer, shared predictive gain, and integrated learning gain; three calibrated laws and direct early-frontier forecasts give 36 fixed combinations/rules/baselines. Balanced-SVD files provide a common measured codebook target, with calibration selection and separate test verification. A container-only baseline tests whether a relation mostly predicts addressing cost. Discovery, development and outer audits remain separate; this old panel cannot supply a genuinely prospective scaling claim.

The strongest outcome would be a stable predictive construction that survives these distinctions. An informative failure would identify whether the limiting factor is learner access, the encoder's gap from a cheaper functional implementation, target instability, or transfer of the calibration. No winning measure is established yet.
