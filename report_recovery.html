Denoising or shrinkage?

A LoRA trained on deliberately mismatched explanations loses two thirds of its GSM8K accuracy. Compress it to rank 1 at one bit and it beats the model it was damaged from. Two studies on what is actually happening — and four hypotheses that died on the way.

Qwen2.5-7B base, nf4MetaMathQA, rationales permuted across problems GSM8K, 500 questionsone training seedupnquick GPU 0 & 1

The short answer

The recovery is shrinkage. Scaling the damaged update down by a tuned constant works as well as compressing it — the coded adapter has no advantage over the best scale (−0.6 points, 95% CI [−2.8, +1.6]).

But compression removes corruption more efficiently. Held to the same update norm, the coded adapter beats the rescaled one at all four bit widths, and it retains the least corruption per unit of update of anything tested.

And what survives is not task knowledge. It is cosine 0.006 to an adapter trained on clean data that scores the same. What compression leaves behind is a chain-of-thought format prior: write one reasoning step per line. That is worth +5.4 points, because this model's arithmetic was never the bottleneck — its step-tracking was.

The damage and the format prior are separable in the spectrum — and not the way round you would guess. The corruption sits in the dominant singular directions. Discard the top four and keep the tail at full precision, with no compression at all, and you get +8.0 points — the best result in the project.

Part oneIs the recovery just making the update smaller?

Quantization shrinks an update as well as coarsening it. The recorded result varied precision at a single rank, so a plain rescaling of the same damaged update could in principle reproduce all of it. This is that control.

Three operations that differ completely in kind land in the same place. Coarse coding at rank one, a scalar multiplying the rank-one direction, and a scalar multiplying the whole rank-16 update — none of which involves discarding anything — all recover the base model and overshoot it by five to six points.

Against a tuned scale, compression buys nothing claim refuted

If you are allowed to pick the scale, you do not need the codec. The best point on the α curve reaches 80.8%; the one-bit coded adapter reaches 80.2%. That difference is noise.

Against a scale matched to its own norm, it buys a little holds

The fair comparison holds magnitude fixed: the same rank-one directions, rescaled to exactly the norm the codec produced — matched to a ratio of 1.000000. There the codec wins at every bit width tested, three of four intervals excluding zero.

Compression is a more efficient way to remove corruption holds

The denoising question is not "is there less corruption" — turning any update down achieves that — but "is there less corruption for the same amount of update". Corruption here is measured directly: reasoning-span likelihood on 256 paired training rows, the permuted rationale against its aligned counterpart, same prompt and same answer line.

And it beats scaling even when scaling has the magnitude advantage holds

The likelihood gaps above were point estimates, because the first pass stored only a token-weighted aggregate. Recomputing them one row at a time makes the row the unit of analysis and lets the paired difference be bootstrapped — and it turns the efficiency argument into something stronger than a ratio.

The last two rows are the limit of the claim: neither reaches the base model's cleanliness. Compression denoises substantially, not completely.

It does not move toward the clean solution claim refuted

An adapter trained on the aligned rationales — identical in every other field — reaches 80.4%, statistically the same as the compressed damaged one. It is not, however, the same object, and compression moves away from it rather than toward it.

Part twoWhat is the surviving direction actually doing?

The compressed adapter writes 45% more words than the base model and almost exactly the same number of arithmetic operations. The obvious reading is that it thinks longer and longer is better. Four experiments say otherwise.

It is not length hypothesis dead

Two instruments lengthen the base model's chain with no adapter attached: a constant negative bias on the answer-marker tokens, and a hard floor forbidding it to finish. At matched chain length the forced model scores 69.4% and the adapter 80.2% — a gap of +10.8 points, 95% CI [+6.6, +15.0], with length held equal. Neither instrument ever helps.

The two arms also get there in opposite ways. Forcing length makes the model write more computations at the same verbal density. The adapter holds the computation count fixed and raises the density — the only condition tested that does.

It is not a stopping prior either hypothesis dead

Teacher-forcing the base model's own chains under both weight settings gives the update's real effect on the decision to stop: −0.13 nats, roughly uniform along the chain. That is four times smaller than the weakest bias swept above, which produced a tenth as much lengthening. Per nat of suppression the adapter lengthens the chain about 37 times as much. The arithmetic rules out the termination logit as the mediator.

What it does do: it breaks lines holds

Differencing the expected frequency of every token, across 14,383 chain positions, gives a dominant answer. Two tokens account for about two thirds of all the probability mass the update moves, and they are the same event:

The update turns sentence-ending periods into line breaks. It rewrites the chain of thought from a running paragraph into one computation per line. The base model segments 14% of its sentences; the adapter segments 93%.

But copying that average does not copy the benefit holds

Putting the update's own measured per-token shift back onto the base model — its top 80 tokens, the first-order reconstruction of its effect on the output distribution — recovers 37% of the lengthening and 7% of the accuracy gain. Scaling that shift up does not rescue it: the best point is +1.8 [−0.6, +4.2], and beyond 3× the intervention starts splitting decimal numbers across line breaks and collapses.

The reason is in the ambiguity of the token itself. A "." ends a sentence and separates a decimal. Segmenting reasoning steps means telling those apart — and a constant logit shift cannot, even in principle.

Part threeWeights, or something a prompt could do?

Retraction — a conclusion I published mid-investigation and then overturned

An earlier version of this note concluded that the effect was "genuinely weight-level, not reducible to an in-context prior", on the strength of an experiment where MetaMath-register exemplars lost 4.2 points against a GSM8K-register control.

That experiment was confounded: the two exemplar sets differed in problem distribution as well as register, because MetaMathQA problems are not GSM8K problems. Repeating it with the three exemplar problems held fixed reverses the result. The conclusion was wrong and is withdrawn.

With only the register varying — terse solutions being GSM8K's own, segmented ones being what the adapter itself wrote for those same three problems, so neither is authored by hand — three demonstrations in the prompt reproduce the entire adapter gain, with no adapter attached.

So the dividing line is not weights against context. It is conditioning against no conditioning. A demonstration conveys when to segment; a constant bias can only say whether. Interventions in the first group reproduce the benefit; interventions in the second reproduce the form and none of it.

Two caveats that keep this honest. Segmentation alone is not sufficient: the terse exemplars also induce it — 64.6%, since gold GSM8K solutions are themselves newline-separated — and buy only +1.0. And the adapter and the prompt are not the same function despite identical accuracy: of the items each repairs over base (64 and 58), only 41 overlap, a Jaccard of 0.51.

What does the direction actually read? holds

A rank-one update writes b·(a·x), so every token carries one scalar per module saying how strongly the direction fires there. If this is a step-boundary detector, that coefficient should differ where a step is about to end from where it is not. Measured on the base model's own chains, so the text is identical across the positions being compared:

The direction reads step boundaries about eleven times more strongly than chance, in two thirds of modules, with module-specific sign. The first version of this measurement used the absolute coefficient and reported nothing — a direction that writes +b at a boundary and −b elsewhere is perfectly selective and has an absolute ratio of exactly one.

The depth profile is informative by disagreeing with the causal one. Layers 21–27 detect step boundaries as strongly as any band and are worth exactly zero points, which is what you would expect: by layer 25 there is no downstream computation left to use the information. Detecting a boundary and being useful are separable, and only the early layers have both.

Part fourWhere in the update does the damage live?

Compression keeps the top of the spectrum and drops the rest, which invites the reading that the top direction is signal and the tail is memorised noise. Slicing the spectrum tests it — and reverses it.

A prefix always contains the top direction, so only the middle and tail bands can falsify the claim. Each band is evaluated twice: kept at full precision, and put through a one-bit file.

The corruption is concentrated in the dominant directions. Keeping the top eight at full precision reproduces the damaged adapter almost exactly (22.6% against 23.0%). Keeping only the tail — discarding 73% of the update's energy, including everything low-rank compression would have preserved — gives 82.8%, or +8.0 points over base, 95% CI [+4.2, +12.0]. That is above the compressed adapter and above the clean-trained adapter, with no compression anywhere.

In hindsight it is what training should produce. Every training row carried a mismatched rationale, so the corruption was a strong and consistent gradient signal, and strong consistent signals are what the dominant directions absorb. The format prior is the weaker, more diffuse residue.

And compression helps exactly where there is damage to destroy. Every band containing the dominant directions gains between +34 and +48 points from being coded at one bit. The tail — the one band containing none of them — loses 6.8, with an interval that excludes zero. Five bands, one rule. Rank-1 truncation keeps precisely the most corrupted component of the update, and survives only because one-bit coding mangles it past the point of doing harm.

One more thing this rules out. rank 1 @ fp16 segments 81.6% of its sentences — nearly as much as the full adapter's 93% — and scores 40.2%. So segmentation is not sufficient either: the benefit needs the segmentation and the corruption gone, and the top direction carries both at once.

Part sixWhat this does not establish

One training seed, one model family, one corruption, one dataset pair. Seed variance is the main untested threat to every effect above.

"Register" describes behaviour, not a circuit. The effect is redundant across the lower half of the stack — layers 0–6 alone are indistinguishable from all 28 — so there is nothing to point at. And knowing the decision must be conditional does not say what it conditions on.

Five instrument bugs were found and fixed during this work — a wrong-corpus join, exemplars truncated before their answer line, few-shot run-on contaminating answer extraction, log-probabilities averaged where probabilities were needed, and an absolute coefficient measured where a signed one was required. Each produced a confident, plausible number before it was caught, and two of them reported a null that was not there. Given that rate, treat any single un-cross-checked measurement here as provisional.

Norm is the matched quantity, and that is a choice. A scalar is exactly what moves Frobenius norm, which is why it is the right control here, but a reader who thinks the relevant magnitude is something else is not answered by this grid.

Intervals are unadjusted for the number of conditions, and several sweeps were chosen adaptively — the bias grid after the hard floor overshot, the scaled grid after the unscaled one moved nothing.

Methods. Qwen2.5-7B base on an nf4 backbone, LoRA rank 16 on all linear projections, α/rank = 2, trained one epoch on 32,000 MetaMathQA rows whose worked explanation was taken from a different problem. Accuracies are exact match on a fixed 500-question GSM8K slice; intervals are paired bootstraps over items, 10,000 draws. Corruption is reasoning-span negative log-likelihood on 256 paired training rows. Update norms carry a common α/rank factor and are comparable across conditions rather than absolute.
